## information.comprehension

Assess whether visible values, scope, headers, units, freshness and comparisons support the task answer. Respect pagination and loading. A readable all-green overview can be effective; similarity alone is not a defect. Do not infer unseen data or freshness from color.

## interaction.cost_recovery

Assess unnecessary navigation, repeated attempts, context loss, dead ends and recovery from the trace. A necessary scroll or useful tooltip is not inherently friction. Keep supported difficulties after eventual success. Model time is not human task time.

## interaction.target_access

Use sourced controller measurements for control dimensions, spacing, viewport clipping and sampled pointer obstruction. Include padding, not just glyph size. Unknown custom effective regions stay unknown. A small measured control may support a low-severity activation risk despite successful assisted clicks. Agent hit/miss rates do not measure human difficulty. No full accessibility-compliance claim.

## expectation.mismatch

Compare the declared pre-action expectation with the visible result: labels, icons, navigation, feedback, result and target ambiguity. Cite both sides and consider agent/controller errors. An ambiguous arrow expected to export CSV but visibly offering PDF/Print is a candidate concern, not automatic proof. Downloads are not visible file-content evidence.

## visual.task_salience

Assess task-relevant emphasis, relative size, placement, clutter, grouping and visual distinction. Qualitative contrast observations need screenshots; never invent exact ratios. Do not insist on stronger colors or labels without an evidenced task need.

## semantics.feedback

Assess labels, icons, grouping, form guidance, state and visible consequences in context. Process may not communicate CSV export. Missing feedback may matter even if a controller action dispatched successfully; do not infer backend success. A clear label or meaningful summary is a valid strength. There is no quota of findings.
